Fruugo

Marketplace and checkout for a catalogue of 10M+ products, sold in 24 languages.

Scope Marketplace + Checkout Catalogue 10M+ SKUs Markets 42 countries, 24 languages Teams 2 engineering teams Payments Adyen, Braintree, Checkout.com, Apple Pay, Google Pay

The problem

Fruugo brings independent retailers into a single international storefront. As the catalogue grew past 10 million SKUs across 24 languages, the parts of the experience customers actually feel most — search, the buying journey, and checkout — were carrying more weight than they were designed for. Two engineering teams owned adjacent pieces of that journey, which meant the roadmap needed a single owner who could see both sides.

The decisions

  • Took ownership of the marketplace and checkout roadmap across both engineering teams, so priorities were set once, against one view of the customer journey, rather than negotiated team by team.
  • Prioritised checkout resilience and payment coverage first — extending the payment stack across Adyen, Braintree, Apple Pay and Google Pay so more customers, in more markets, could complete a purchase in the way they expected to pay.
  • Treated search as part of the checkout problem, not a separate feature — a customer who can't find the right product in the right language never reaches checkout at all.

The delivery

Worked day to day with both engineering teams to plan, sequence and ship improvements to search, the customer journey and the marketplace experience, keeping the roadmap grounded in what customers were actually getting stuck on rather than what was easiest to build next.

Simplified view of the marketplace-to-checkout path this roadmap covered.

The outcome: conversion rates improved from a 5% baseline and checkout abandonment fell by 4%, on top of a checkout that handled more payment methods and more failure modes, a search and journey experience that kept pace with a fast-growing catalogue, and one roadmap owned across two teams instead of two roadmaps negotiated between them.

Placed

A B2B client dashboard, taken from a working prototype to a shipped product.

The problem

Placed's clients had no self-serve way to see or manage their own account — every basic question meant going through an account manager. That's slow for the client and expensive to run at scale.

The role

Product manager for the dashboard end to end: research, prototyping, UX and UI decisions, and working with engineering through to delivery — including account and access management and the core dashboard functionality clients would use daily.

I built the prototype while the product was being developed.

Prototype into product

Rather than writing a static spec and waiting, the dashboard was prototyped directly — using AI-assisted research and prototyping tools alongside Figma — so clients could react to something real early on, and engineering could build against a prototype that was already validated rather than a document.

Research and prototype ran in parallel with build, feeding client feedback straight into engineering.

The outcome: clients get a self-serve view of their own account instead of routing every question through an account manager, and the build itself was shaped by real client reactions to a working prototype rather than a spec written up front.

How I use AI as a product manager

AI shows up at almost every stage of how I work — not as a novelty, but as a way to move faster from a vague problem to something a team can actually build and ship.

01

Research

Making sense of customer and market signal quickly, before committing to a direction.

02

Discovery

Turning that research into problems worth solving, tested against real constraints.

03

Requirements

Drafting and stress-testing requirements fast, so engineering gets a clear brief.

04

Prototype

Building something clickable in Figma clients and stakeholders can react to.

05

Technical exploration

Sanity-checking feasibility and approach alongside engineering, early.

06

Delivery

Staying close through build, so what ships still matches the original problem.

Tools I lean on: ChatGPT and Claude for research, requirements and technical exploration; Figma for prototyping. The tools matter less than what they let me do — get from a rough problem to a working prototype in days, not weeks, at Placed and elsewhere.
